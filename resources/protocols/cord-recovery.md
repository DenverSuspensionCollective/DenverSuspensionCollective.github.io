*Procedures for recovering, decontaminating and donating single-use suspension cord.*

{% include heading_block.html title="Purpose" %}

All cord used in a suspension is single use. Instead of sending usable cord to the trash, DSC recovers it, decontaminates it and donates it to creative reuse organizations. Recovered cord is never returned to suspension use. This protocol works alongside the <a href="{% link resources/protocols/infection-control.md %}">Infection Control</a> and <a href="{% link resources/protocols/rigging.md %}">Rigging</a> protocols.

{% include heading_block.html title="Definitions" %}

1. **Recoverable Cord:** any single-use suspension cord, or segment of it, that is at least 1 yard (36 inches) long and has no visible blood or OPIM.

1. **OPIM:** other potentially infectious material, including body fluids other than blood.

1. **Visibly Contaminated Cord:** any segment of cord with visible blood or OPIM. Visibly contaminated segments are cut out and discarded. The rest of the cord from the same suspension can still be recovered if it passes inspection.

1. **Cord Recovery Bin:** a red bio-waste bag inside a labeled 5-gallon bucket, used only for recoverable cord.

1. **Decontamination:** the inspection, washing and disinfecting steps in this protocol. Under the Infection Control protocol, all cord is considered contaminated once it is connected to the suspendee, so every piece of recovered cord goes through decontamination before it is donated.

1. **Receiving Organization:** a creative reuse organization that has agreed in writing to accept DSC cord donations.

{% include heading_block.html title="Collection at the Event" %}

Exam gloves are sufficient PPE for collection.

1. Don exam gloves.

1. Remove and wipe the shackles as described in <a href="{% link resources/protocols/infection-control.md %}#processing-hardware">Processing Hardware</a> in the Infection Control protocol.

1. Inspect the cord. Cut out any segment with visible blood or OPIM and dispose of it in the contaminated waste bin. The rest of the cord from the same suspension can still be recovered.

1. Cut the remaining cord at the knots, or untie the knots if convenient. Make no other cuts, so the lengths stay as long as possible. Discard any piece shorter than 1 yard in the contaminated waste bin.

1. Place the cut lengths in the cord recovery bin.

1. Change exam gloves and continue processing the rig as described in the Infection Control protocol.

1. At the end of the event, close the red bag with a knot and keep it in the labeled bucket for transport.

{% include heading_block.html title="Transport and Storage" %}

1. Keep the closed bag in the labeled bucket during transport and storage.

1. Store the bucket away from food and clean laundry.

1. Begin decontamination within 48 hours of the event.

{% include heading_block.html title="Decontamination and Washing" %}

#### Materials

- Exam gloves
- Mesh laundry bags
- Lysol Laundry Sanitizer, with pre-soak directions on the label for HIV-1, HBV and HCV
- Regular laundry detergent
- A clean basin or bucket large enough to fully submerge the mesh bags
- A clean weight to hold the cord under water
- A clean drying rack or surface

#### Inspection

1. Don exam gloves. Keep them on until the disinfecting soak and machine wash are complete.

1. Open the bag and inspect each piece of cord again. Cut out any segment with visible blood or OPIM, and discard any piece shorter than 1 yard. Seal discarded cord in a bag and dispose of it as contaminated waste.

1. Load the cord loosely into mesh laundry bags. Do not overfill them, so that wash water and disinfectant reach all of the cord and tangling is limited.

#### Pre-wash

1. Wash the mesh bags in the washing machine on a cold cycle with regular detergent. Do not include any other laundry.

The pre-wash removes soil before disinfection. The disinfectant label requires items to be cleaned before it is applied, and cold water avoids setting any protein residue.

#### Disinfecting Soak

1. In the clean basin, mix Lysol Laundry Sanitizer with cold water at the dilution given in the label's pre-soak directions (currently 2 capfuls to line 1 per gallon of water). Make enough solution to cover the mesh bags completely.

1. Submerge the mesh bags. Press and squeeze them under the surface to release trapped air. UHMWPE floats and sheds water, so place the clean weight on top to keep all of the cord under the surface.

1. Leave the cord fully submerged for at least 15 minutes.

#### Wash

1. Move the mesh bags to the washing machine and wash on a normal cold cycle with detergent. Add Lysol Laundry Sanitizer to the rinse dispenser as directed on the label.

1. Pour the used soak solution down a drain and rinse and dry the basin.

1. Before the machine is used for other laundry, wipe the door seal and run an empty cleaning cycle following the manufacturer's instructions.

1. Remove gloves and wash your hands. Decontamination is now complete, and the cord can be handled without gloves.

#### Drying

1. Take the cord out of the mesh bags and spread it out on the clean drying rack or surface.

1. Air dry until the cord is completely dry, including the core of braided cord and the inside of any coils. Do not use a dryer.

1. Cut and gently burn any frayed ends to avoid further fraying.

#### Final Inspection

1. Check every piece. It must be completely dry, free of mildew and free of visible staining.

1. Cut out any segment that fails this check, and discard any piece shorter than 1 yard.

{% include heading_block.html title="Packing and Shipping" %}

1. Coil the cord loosely and pack it in clean bags.

1. Follow the receiving organization's packing requirements.

1. Include a copy of the [DSC cord donation letter](#appendix-cord-donation-letter) in each package.

1. Ship only to a receiving organization that has confirmed acceptance in writing.

{% include heading_block.html title="General Cord Recovery Guidelines" %}

1. Recovered cord is never used again for suspension.

1. All cord is considered contaminated once it is connected to the suspendee. Handle all recovered cord with gloves until decontamination is complete.

1. When in doubt, discard. Any segment with visible blood or OPIM, at any stage, is cut out and discarded.

1. Follow the product label. Where the label and this protocol differ, the label directions take precedence.

1. Do not mix Lysol Laundry Sanitizer with bleach or any other disinfectant.

1. Never wash recovered cord with other laundry.
